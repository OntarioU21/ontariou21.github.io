How the Ratings Work

Two numbers go into every rating: how much a team wins or loses by, and how good its opponents are.

Rating = AGD + SCHED

AGD: Average Goal Differential

For each game we take goals for minus goals against, then average it over the season. A single game can only count for up to 7 goals either way, so a 12-0 win counts the same as a 7-0 win. Running up the score doesn't help. Games decided in a shootout count as ties.

SCHED: Schedule Strength

SCHED is the average rating of every opponent a team has played. Beating good teams is worth more than beating weak ones, and losing close to a strong team can still help your rating.

Everything is solved at once

Your rating depends on your opponents' ratings, which depend on their opponents, and so on. The site solves the whole set of games together so that every team's rating is exactly its AGD plus its SCHED at the same time. Every game played by any team ends up affecting everyone connected to it.

What the numbers mean

Only the gap between two ratings matters. If Team A is rated 95.50 and Team B is 93.00, Team A is expected to win by about 2.5 goals. The scale is shifted so the top ranked team is at 99.99. Each game also gets a game rating (goal differential plus the opponent's rating), which shows how well a team played that night compared to its season average.

Moneyline odds

The rating gap is the expected goal margin, but real games scatter around it. The site measures how far results usually land from the prediction (about 2.6 goals) and how often games end tied (12%, shootouts included). From those two numbers it works out each team's chance to win, tie or lose, then converts that chance to American odds. A favourite with a 75% chance is -300, and an underdog with a 25% chance is +300.

The standard moneyline treats a tie as a push. The 3-way line prices the tie as its own outcome. Odds start out fair (no bookmaker margin), and the predictor lets you add vig. These odds are for analysis and fun. Ratings only know final scores, not lineups, injuries or travel.

The blowout rule

If two teams are more than 7 goals apart in rating and the stronger team wins by 7 or more, the game is left out for both teams. The favourite did everything it could, and because of the cap it would otherwise be punished for it. If the favourite wins by less than expected, the game still counts.

Who gets ranked

Teams need at least 10 games before they get a rank. Teams with fewer games still have a rating, which you can see by ticking "Show teams under the game minimum". Ratings are most reliable when tiers and leagues play each other in tournaments and exhibitions, because those games link the different groups of teams together. A team that has never played anyone linked to the main group is marked as not connected.

Which games count

League, playoff, tournament and exhibition games all count the same, as long as a final score has been entered.
